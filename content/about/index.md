Htun Teza is a Burmese data scientist and dentist based in Bangkok, Thailand. He trained as a dental surgeon at the University of Dental Medicine Yangon — a six-year programme that also laid the foundation for his interest in oral epidemiology and public health — before moving to Thailand to pursue a Master of Science in Data Science for Healthcare at Mahidol University. His thesis applied machine learning models, including mixed-effects logistic regression and recurrent neural networks, to the diagnosis of chronic periodontitis using longitudinal cohort data from the Electricity Generating Authority of Thailand study.

He is currently a Graduate Research Assistant at the Department of Clinical Epidemiology and Biostatistics, Faculty of Medicine Ramathibodi Hospital, Mahidol University, affiliated with the Data Science and Clinical Informatics division. The work spans the full research data pipeline: building and maintaining the CEB Data Warehouse, extracting and engineering electronic health records into analysis-ready datasets, harmonising and imputing data across large real-world cohorts, and applying statistical and machine learning methods to epidemiological and methodological questions. In parallel, he mentors and collaborates on multidisciplinary studies involving cardiovascular disease, chronic kidney disease, diabetes, and oral health.

His research interests centre on the challenges that arise when routine clinical data is repurposed for research. This includes questions of data observability, biases inherent in or introduced through data integration, and imputation strategies for missing data at scale. He is particularly drawn to multi-state modelling, risk prediction, and real-world evidence studies — areas where the gap between clinical messiness and methodological rigour is widest and most interesting. His tools of choice are Python, R, Stata, and SPSS.
